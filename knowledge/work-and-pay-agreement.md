# Motor Work and Pay Agreement (Hire Purchase Contract)

This is the authoritative source for RideIQ's Work & Pay contract terms.

## The arrangement

The Owner, Charles Nnamdi, provides a brand-new Royal 125 motorcycle to a Worker under a Work-and-Pay (hire purchase) arrangement. The motorcycle is valued at GHS 11,400. The Worker uses the motorcycle to work and earn an income, and repays the Owner over time. The motorcycle stays the Owner's property until the Worker has paid in full.

## Payment terms

- The Worker repays a total of GHS 20,000.
- The Worker pays in weekly installments of GHS 400.
- GHS 20,000 paid at GHS 400 a week is 50 weekly payments.
- 50 weekly payments is about 11 months and 2 weeks.
- The Worker has a maximum of 12 months (about 52 weeks) to finish paying.
- Each weekly payment is due on the same day every week, the day the contract started. If the first payment is due on a Monday, every payment is due on a Monday.

## Weeks in months

Weeks and months, in plain terms (a month is about 4 weeks and 1 day):

- 4 weeks is about 1 month.
- 13 weeks is about 3 months.
- 26 weeks is about 6 months.
- 39 weeks is about 9 months.
- 48 weeks is about 11 months.
- 50 weeks is about 11 months and 2 weeks.
- 52 weeks is 12 months.

## Owning the motorcycle (early completion)

If the Worker pays the full GHS 20,000 within 11 months, the motorcycle automatically becomes his own personal property.

## If the Worker does not finish paying (non-completion)

If the Worker does not pay the full GHS 20,000 within 12 months, the motorcycle is taken back (repossessed) and the agreement ends.

## Late payments and the grace rule

- A payment is late when it is not made on the exact day it is due. For example, if the payment is due on Monday and the Worker brings it on Tuesday, that payment is late.
- Each late payment counts as one strike against the Worker.
- The Worker may combine the missed week's payment with the next week's payment.
- A late payment counts as a strike even if the Worker pays the next day. Making the payment up later does not erase the strike. Strikes are permanent for the life of the agreement.
- The Worker is allowed no more than three strikes. On the third late payment, the motorcycle is repossessed. The number of late payments must never go past three.
- The strikes are shown in the app as "Grace used". 1 of 3, 2 of 3, then repossession on the third.

## Maintenance and repairs

The Worker is responsible for all maintenance and repair costs of the motorcycle, at his own cost. This includes changing tires, repairing accident damage, changing engine oil, and replacing lights or brakes.

## Illness

If the Worker falls ill and cannot work, he must return the motorcycle to the Owner's residence. He may take the motorcycle back once he has recovered and is fit to work again.

## Repossession and recovering the motorcycle

- If the motorcycle is taken back (repossessed) and it is damaged, the Worker is responsible for the cost of the repairs.
- If the Worker has an unresolved debt to the Owner, the matter can be reported to the police.
- If the Worker cannot produce or locate the motorcycle, he must provide a replacement brand-new motorcycle. If he does not, he faces the same police report and liability for the debt.

## Guarantor

- The Worker must bring a family member to act as Guarantor.
- If the Worker defaults and cannot be found, the Guarantor becomes responsible for the debt and can be reported to the police.

## Identification

Both the Worker and the Guarantor must provide valid identification (for example a Ghana Card) when signing. Their ID details are recorded on the agreement.
